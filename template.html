About

I build physics-based and AI-accelerated simulators for the solid Earth, with a focus on AI for physical system simulation: user-friendly, reproducible, reliable and parallel open-source scientific software in geoscience and beyond.

A seismologist by training, I model earthquake rupture, multicycle fault behavior and ground shaking with parallel finite-element methods, and integrate those models with paleoseismic, geodetic and ground-motion observations. I joined the University of Texas Institute for Geophysics as its first hire dedicated to computational science, bridging geophysical research and high-performance computing. My work also spans geodynamics, InSAR, planetary interiors and climate modeling.

I believe software development should be driven by scientific questions, and that clean, tested, continuously refactored code frees scientists to do science.

Research

Earthquake rupture, cycles and ground motion

Scenario earthquakes and ground shaking with EQdyna; observation-constrained multicycle dynamics for the southern San Andreas and Altyn Tagh faults; rate-and-state earthquake cycles on complex fault geometries with EQquasi; ground shaking over many cycles with the EQsimu coupling infrastructure; community code-verification benchmarks (SCEC).

EQdyna · EQquasi · EQsimu · EQdyna.2Dcycle

Machine-learning surrogates for physical simulation

Graph neural network simulators that learn earthquake rupture dynamics from finite-element runs (EQGNS), and nonlinearly coupled permafrost dynamics from graph networks.

EQGNS · GNN surrogates

Geodynamics and inversion

An analytical and numerical toolset to constrain viscous anisotropy; mixed forward/inverse frameworks for faulting and adjoint-based joint inversion for material properties, including volcanic-arc rigidity from coseismic deformation.

FEniCS · adjoint methods

InSAR

Development of GMTSAR for next-generation InSAR satellites, and coupling InSAR with finite-element models to resolve compliant fault damage zones.

GMTSAR

Planetary interiors

Interior models of Mercury and the conditions for iron snow in its core, with the PIE inversion code.

PIE

Climate modeling

CESM simulations of past and future El Niño behavior and aerosol–climate interactions.

CESM
